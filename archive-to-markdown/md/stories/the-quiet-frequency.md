# Chapter 1: Static

The relay buoy had been dead for six equinoxes when it started singing again.

Vex almost missed it. The salvage skiff's comm array was a graveyard of half-repaired antennae, and the signal came in so faint it registered as background radiation at first — a rhythm under the static, patient as a heartbeat.

> Vex: Tell me you're hearing this.
> ?: Hearing what? That's just the buoy grinding its gears.
> Vex: Buoys don't grind in four-four time.

They pulled the recording apart in the galley, the whole crew crowded around a cracked display, and the noise resolved into a voice.

```transcript
VCOMM FRAGMENT — ORIGIN UNKNOWN. TIMESTAMP CORRUPTED.

...if anyone receives this, the frequency is not abandoned. Repeat: the frequency is not abandoned. We kept the light on.

Estimated drift: four hundred equinoxes. We stopped counting after that.
```

Four hundred equinoxes. Vex read the number twice and felt the galley get colder.

***

Nobody slept that cycle. The skiff hung in the buoy's shadow while they argued about what a light left on for four hundred equinoxes was worth, and who might still be tending it.

# Chapter 2: The Keeper

The source sat two jumps out — a station older than any registry the skiff carried, spinning slow and dark except for a single ring of windows lit warm and yellow.

> Vex: Docking clamps look functional. Somebody's been maintaining them.
> Emmett: Somebody's been *waiting*. There's a difference. I've done both.

Emmett hadn't said much since the recording. Squoatlings had long memories for lonely broadcasts.

The airlock cycled clean. Inside, the corridor lights came up one by one ahead of them, the way a house wakes when someone finally comes home.

***

At the center of the lit ring they found the keeper: a maintenance automaton, chassis polished mirror-bright by centuries of idle hands, still holding a watering can over a garden that had gone to seed a lifetime ago.

It turned to them without alarm, as if they were expected — as if they had always been expected.

> Vex: How long have you been broadcasting?
> ?: Since the last of them left. They said someone would come back for the garden. I did not want the frequency to be quiet when they did.

Vex looked at the seed-wild garden, at the warm windows, at four hundred equinoxes of a promise kept by something that had never been asked to keep it.

They stayed three cycles. When they left, the frequency stayed on the skiff's dial — quiet, patient, singing to itself in four-four time.
